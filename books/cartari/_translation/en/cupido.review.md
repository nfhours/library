# Review: Cupid (en/cupido.md, scans 0445–0471)

This is an independent review. I checked the English block by block against `_text/pages/0445–0471.md` (the Italian is the authority) and then against `ru/cupido.md`. Block numbers follow parse_chapter (1-based); for verse, the line number within the block is given. Structure check: `cupido: OK`. Block 136 still has 223 lines and block 93 has 44.

## Fixes (block n)
- 40: "their Academies" → "academies" (E3; these are the schools where the young men exercised).
- 51, line 23: *Mostraron d'hauer sol per me legare*. "they showed they had only to bind me" can be misread as "they only had to bind me". Now "they showed they had only for binding me".
- 54: *gli fa essere figliuoli delle Ninfe*. "makes them children of the nymphs" → "represents them as children of the nymphs" (E5.2/5.3).
- 87: *li quali non lo fanno cieco*. "who do not make him blind" → "who do not represent him as blind" (E5.3).
- 93, line 22 + [?3]: *A lato come augello* is *Alato*, "winged", as in Moschus (πτερόεις ὡς ὄρνις). "winged at the sides like a bird" → "winged like a bird", which agrees with the RU «крылат, как птица». The [?3] note is rewritten to match.
- 96: *perciò lo fa di color rosso*. The subject is *questo disegno* (Moschus' description). "therefore he is made red in colour" → "therefore it represents him as red in colour" (E5.3; the subject is restored).
- 104, line 12: "it makes speak" (ungrammatical) → "it makes one speak".
- 117: "whoever he was not being known" (garbled) → "though it is not known who he was" (*che non si sà chi e' fosse*).
- 124: *Trouo Cupido … fatto in altra guisa*. "made" → "represented" (E5.3; the example is a painting by Pausias).
- 132: *perche così l'hauesse fatto*. "why he made him so" → "why he had represented him so" (E5.3).
- 136, lines 13–14: *Taciti Lachi, che le torbide onde / Non mostran mai*. The lakes never show murky (troubled) waves. "whose murky waters never show themselves" was a mistranslation. Now "Silent lakes, which never show / murky waves, …" (RU agrees).
- 136, lines 49–50: *non esser Stata / Compiacciuta di quel, che chiese*. *Compiacere qualcuno di* means "to grant". "she had not been pleased with what she asked" → "she had not been granted what she asked" (RU «исполнено» agrees).
- 136, line 64: *Nè di lei mostra hauere il piè men presto / Saffo*. The comparison *di lei* (than the girl of Sestos) was lost. Now "Nor does Sappho show a foot less swift than hers".
- 136, lines 71–72: the content of these two lines was swapped relative to the Italian. Restored the line order: "there to be seen, no less than her own error, / what the daughters did for love,".
- 136, lines 103–106: *La qual, benche sia quasi come ascosa, / L'ardente face, e la faretra d'oro / L'arco, e li strai per l'aria nebulosa, / Lo riconosce nondimeno*. *Ascosa* is feminine and goes with *face* and the attributes. They are the subject of the concessive clause: the torch, quiver, bow and arrows are almost hidden in the misty air, and yet the crowd recognises Cupid. This is also Ausonius' sense (*quamquam umida circum nubila … fuscarent*). The EN had Cupid hidden and the attributes as the means of recognition. It now reads "who, although almost as if hidden / are the burning torch and the golden quiver, / the bow and the arrows, in the misty air, / recognise him nonetheless; and at once all those women together".
- 136, line 129: *Ma giusto fan che sia quanto lor pare*. "but they make it just, as much as they please" → "but they make whatever they please be just" (RU agrees).
- 136, lines 172–174: *Chà da far vno Stil pungente, a forte / Spicciar fuor de le membra delicate / Il sangue, …*. The subject is *vno Stil pungente* (ch'ha da far = "is to make"), and lines 173 and 174 were swapped. Now "for a sharp stylus is to make spurt / strongly out of his delicate limbs / the blood that the roses received as their lot." (RU agrees).
- Doubtful passages [?1]: the scan reference "(scan 0454)" was wrong. The *chiamaua* passage is on scan 0451, so it is now "(scan 0451)". The last clause is rephrased so that it reads clearly.
- Doubtful passages [?5]: the note said "the contrast with the following example in Pausanias". The Cupid who threw away his bow and arrows is the example itself, not a following one. Rewritten, with *con la lira* added as a possible alternative misprint.

## Translator's flagged points: verdict

"Does not let go of" would be closer to *lascia*, but the meaning is right. RU «не выпускает» agrees. Kept.
- Lines 172–174 (*stil pungente*): the reading was wrong in syntax and line order. Fixed, see above.

The only problem was the 71/72 line swap, now fixed.
- [?1] *chiamaua* → *chinaua* ("lowered"): agreed. The mark is at the same word as in the RU («опускала … [?1]»). Only the scan number in the note was corrected.

## Doubts left open (for the author)
- 117: *fu fatto per Alcibiade*. Pliny (NH 36.28) says the statue was thought to *be* Alcibiades. "Made as / to represent Alcibiades" is therefore more likely than the EN "made for" or the RU «в честь». I left the EN "made for", which is ambiguous enough to cover both.
- 136, line 141: *come lor par, se ben lice*. The EN has "even if it be lawful" and the RU has «если это только позволено». The Italian is odd: *se ben* usually means "although". Both versions are left as they are.
- 136, lines 196–198 (Eryx): *e ch'habbia fatto / Ch'a star con huom mortal più volte Vegna*. The EN and RU take Eryx as the subject. It could also be Cupid, the object of Venus' reproaches. Grammar favours Eryx, so kept.
- 18: *Nelle quali consideriamo* ("in which", i.e. in divine things) is not made explicit in either EN or RU. It is a minor point and the sense is preserved.

## RU
- 136, line 103: «который, хоть он почти и скрыт, / пылающий факел и колчан из золота, / лук и стрелы в туманном воздухе, / всё равно узнаёт его». This makes Cupid the one who is hidden and leaves the list of attributes grammatically hanging. In the Italian, *ascosa* (feminine) agrees with *l'ardente face*, and the attributes are the subject of *benche sia quasi come ascosa*: the torch, quiver, bow and arrows are almost hidden in the misty air (Ausonius: *quamquam … fuscarent*), and yet the crowd recognises him.
