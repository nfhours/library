# Review: seconda-parte-indiani (EN), checked against the Italian (TEXT_reading.md, scans 0591–0635) and RU

Checker: `seconda-parte-indiani: OK` (65 blocks, same as RU). Block numbers below are the checker's block numbers. The engraving caption `> VOT P R` is **block 31** (not 28). It is correctly a single-line caption.

## English fixes
1. Block 9: the Solinus sentence (~90 words) split into shorter sentences (E1). The lost *poi* ("disse poi molte cose") restored as "afterwards" (E4).
2. Block 23: "Nor do I claim … to force the reader" changed to "Nor do I intend … to coerce the reader". *Pretendo di violentare* means intend/presume, not claim.
3. Block 26: "where at other times the abomination had flourished" changed to "where formerly". *Altre volte* means formerly here, and "at other times" was a calque.
4. Block 26: "So much did … St Benedict, do, who …" changed to "This is just what … St Benedict, did: he …". *Tanto fece* means "the same / just this did", and the English had made it a quantity.
5. Block 27: "the manal stone" changed to "the lapis manalis" (glossary: Pietra manale = lapis manalis).
6. Block 28: "Peter Martyr of Anghiera" changed to "Peter Martyr d'Anghiera" (glossary).
7. Block 29: "apparitions of demons" changed to "apparitions of daemons" (glossary: Demonij = daemons). The singular *Demonio* has no glossary entry. It stays "the Demon" with a capital throughout the chapter (see rulings).
8. Block 34: "Dice, Hooks, Snares, Files, Stars" lowercased (E3: these are common nouns, and the capitals are only the Italian typography).
9. Block 45: "Girolamo Aleandro the Younger, a living and perfect image of the great Cardinal …, the drawing" was ambiguous: "image" could be read as the object that arrived. The apposition is now set off with dashes, so that it clearly describes Aleandro the Younger.
10. Block 46: "the crown, which is of copper coloured gold" changed to "of copper coloured like gold" (*rame colorato d'oro*). The old wording read as "copper-coloured gold".
11. Block 60: "under the feet it is green" changed to "what is under the feet is green". In the Italian the subject of *è verde* is missing (the lacuna), and "it" wrongly made the idol green. [?5] is kept in place.
12. Block 63: "at other times the Japanese had knowledge" changed to "formerly" (*altre volte*, the same calque as in fix 3).
13. Doubtful passages: the Peter Martyr, Dice/Hooks, lapis manalis and [?5] notes updated to match fixes 5, 6, 8 and 11. The [?5] note now gives the probable sense of the lacuna.

## Rulings on the translator's flagged points
- [?1] "que' di Tito": the reading "Tyre" is right. Hiram's Tyrian fleet sailed with Solomon's to Ophir (1 Kings 9:26–28), and the glossary has Tiro = Tyre. Kept as "those of Tyre [?1]".
- [?2] Homopoca: printed once (scan 0596), while Homoyoca is printed twice (0593, 0595). This is almost certainly a misprint. Kept as printed, with [?2], as the EN note says.
- [?5] (scan 0629): the line is corrupt; it is not a missing page. Probable sense: "its flesh colour is of our [colour; the monster] under the feet is green" (compare 0628). It is rendered literally (see fix 11), with no restoration.
- "Demon" vs glossary: the glossary "Daemon/daemons" belongs to the classical *Demone/Demonij*. The singular *Demonio* here is the Christian Devil ("Simia di Dio"), with no glossary entry. The capital "Demon" is kept consistently (blocks 19, 20, 27, 37, 43, 46), as a quasi-proper name. The plural *Demonij* follows the glossary.
- "Rotella" = "Little Shield": acceptable (*rotella* is a small round shield, a buckler). Kept.
- "Hell" for *Inferno* ("Signore dell'Inferno", equated with Lucifer): kept. There is no glossary entry, and the context is Christian.
- Coptos / Taprobane / Ezion-geber / Cadiz / Barreiros: all five identifications are correct.
  - "Capto": the Strabo passage is about the Indian trade route through Coptos.
  - "Taprobana": the Pliny/Solinus story of Annius Plocamus's freedman.
  - "Asion Gaber": the Douay spelling of Ezion-geber (1 Kings 9:26).
  - "Caliz": the old Spanish/Italian form of Cádiz. Eudoxus reached Gades.
  - "Varriero/Variero": Gaspar Barreiros, who put Ophir in the Golden Chersonese/Malacca.
  - None of these has a glossary entry. E2 prefers the established English form, so keep them. They are recorded in glossary-new.tsv.
- Block 31 `> VOT P R`: correct as a single-line caption (it is the only engraving with text).

## Open questions for the author
1. Block 27: "vna nel corpo dall'Incensiere". EN and RU both read this as "on the body, by the Censer". It could instead be a misprint for *dell'Incensiere* ("one on the body of the Censer", the cornucopia). Left as is.
2. Block 34: the glossary has Lucertola = "Lizard" (capitalised, on the Russian convention for animals). EN keeps "a lizard" lowercase under E3. Should the glossary capital apply in English?
3. Theodoret (block 26): the glossary entry "Theodorito" = "Theodoret of Cyrrhus". The text has "Theodoreto" without the epithet, so "Theodoret" is kept (compare the glossary note on Diodorus). Please confirm.
4. "Apolline" (block 26) follows the glossary english column, but the glossary comment suggests "of Apollo". Please confirm for EN.
5. glossary-new.tsv lacks several names used in the chapter: Homoyoca, Gómara, Luís Fróis (printed "Lodouico Fores", an identification rather than the printed form), Pigafetta, Magellan, Pedro de los Ríos, Ximénez, Herwart, Vinghernio, Botero. Please confirm these forms, especially Fróis, against rule E2 and rule 8.
