## Fixes (block n)

### Mistranslations and garbled syntax
- 100 (Dante, Purg. XIX, line 1): *L'altra prendeua* was rendered "The other seized her". The object is *l'altra*: it is the holy woman who seizes the siren. It now reads "She seized the other". The RU «Другую она схватила» is correct.
- 24: "but only so that someone who is like him will, as affairs require" was ungrammatical and lost the sense of *ma da chi solamente è simile à lui*. It now reads "but only by someone who is like him".
- 68: in the proverb *quello, di che egli ha grande abondanza*, *egli* is the receiver (*altrui*), not the giver. "of which they have great abundance" → "of which he already has great abundance".
- 285: *ad essempio di quella la dipinse nel tempio di Diana Efesia* was rendered as if the model were in the temple. The word order is now "painted her, after the model of that one, in the temple of Diana of Ephesus"; *la facesse* → "depicted"; *fece la guerra* → "depicted the war".
- 174: "a certain wallet in which he carried the terrible head he had from some nymphs" read as if the head came from the nymphs. The sentence is now split with a semicolon: the scimitar and the Talaria came from Mercury; the helmet of Orcus and the wallet came from the nymphs (as in the RU and Apollodorus).
- 17: "without the help of his wife" (after "Vulcan opened…") pointed to Vulcan's wife. It now reads "and Minerva was born without the help of Jupiter's wife" (*senza il seruitio della moglie*; Juno, as the sentence goes on).
- 19: *dispositione* → "interpretation" (was "arrangement"); "the one made by" → "given by".
- 103 (Virgil/Pythagoras, line 18): "makes bear him whom she ought least of all to" → "lays upon him on whom she ought least of all to lay them".
- 55 (Claudian, line 3): "has gather" → "gathers" (*fa raccore*).
- 49 (Statius, lines 3–4): "by the strong helmet" had been moved up into line 3. It is back at the start of line 4, as in the Italian (*Il forte elmo*) and the RU. "Numen" → "numen".
- 152: *haueua che fare assai il popolo* → "had a great deal of say" (was "had a great deal to do").
- 66: "acknowledged from her the benefit" → "owed to her the benefit" (*riconoscere da quella*).
- 95: *fatta per l'Honore* → "representing Honour" (was "made for Honour"); "fair blond tresses" → "beautiful blond tresses" (*belle treccie bionde*).
- 37: "they serve nothing for… it also serves little" → "they are of no use for… just as it is of little use".
- 148: "of the sort that we said in the image of Mercury they are" → "of the sort we described in the image of Mercury".
- 169: the dangling "sent to Rome, there was no one" → "when it was sent to Rome, there was no one"; *glie la fecero… hauere morta* → "helped him to get it dead".
- 93: word order: "And a medal of Numerian also has the same figure."
- 135: *il sommo magistrato* → "the supreme magistracy".
- 320: *ciascheduna la sua* (of the eagles) → "each its own".
- 288: *l'Angelo Michele* → "the angel Michael" (was "Archangel"; not in the Italian).

### Unclear pronouns (E4)
- 33: "In her temple" → "In Vesta's temple".
- 172: "because she was the proper numen of that place" → "because Minerva was…". The nearest antecedent was Medusa.
- 183: "And it was sometimes covered [?6]" → "And the head was sometimes covered [?6]" (*coperto*, masc. = *capo*; this agrees with the translator's own note [?6]).
- 191: "he first had their thighs and legs fitted out" → "the thighs and legs of these men" ("their" pointed to the gods).
- 200: "do more than it can" → "more than the body can" (*quanto egli può*, *egli* = *corpo*).
- 231: "having entered his temple" → "Vulcan's temple" ("his" pointed to Cambyses).
- 301: "as though he were to hold him back" → "as though Hercules were to hold Apollo back".

### fare / dipingere = depict (E5.3)
- 12 (Ovid, line 1) *Fà sè* → "She portrays herself"; 82 "painted Truth" → "depicted"; 105 "The image of Honour the ancients made" → "The ancients depicted the image of Honour"; 116 "was made as she was" → "was depicted thus"; 133 "made Terror in two ways" → "depicted"; 146 "Claudian made Minerva's spear" → "depicted"; 161 "made on her breast the Gorgon" → "depicted"; 185 "paints the Trojan war" → "depicts"; 188 "put on it those who" → "depicted on it"; 253 "painted her" → "depicted"; 254 "made her all laden with eyes" → "depicted"; 257 "make like those of a bat" → "depict"; 266 "painted him" → "depicted".

### simulacro = idol (glossary)
- 197, 202, 214, 231, 243, 271, 275, 276, 294, 301, 304, 345: "image(s)"/"statue" for *simulacro/i* → "idol(s)". Aside 274 (*Simulacro di Marte*) → "Idol of Mars". Aside 247 (*Imagine di Marte*) stays "Image of Mars".

### Lower case (E3) and glossary forms
- 27 "Griffins" → "griffins" (an animal, like owl and cock in this chapter); 307 "Maiden" → "maiden"; 161 "Aegis" → "aegis" (glossary, and consistent with 158 and 164); 164 "the earth" → "Earth" (personified: she is begged by the Titans and hides Aex).
- 42: "as Statius when he says" → "as Statius does when he says".

## RU
- No genuine errors of meaning found. The RU has the correct reading in exactly the places where the EN was wrong (100, 24, 174, 285, 17).
