## Second title page (scan 0007)

NEWEST SECOND EDITION
OF THE
IMAGES
OF THE GODS OF THE ANCIENTS
OF VINCENZO CARTARI OF REGGIO.

*Brought back from head to foot to their true likenesses, not observed before.*

*Drawn from marbles, bronzes, medals, gems, and other ancient monuments, with exquisite study and particular diligence*

BY LORENZO PIGNORIA OF PADUA.

*With the Annotations of the same author on the whole work added, and a Discourse on the deities of the East and West Indies, with their figures taken from the originals that are preserved in the galleries of Princes and in the museums of private persons.*

*With the Allegories on the Images, by* CESARE MALFATTI of Padua, *improved and newly enlarged.*
*And a Catalogue of the hundred most famous gods of paganism.*

*With the addition of another Catalogue of the ancient and modern authors who have treated this subject, arranged and compiled by the same Pignoria, who has enlarged the Annotations and added many Images.*

IN PADUA.
At the printing house of Pietro Paolo Tozzi. 1626.

## Dedication (scans 0009–0011)

TO THE MOST ILLUSTRIOUS SIGNOR
and my most honoured patron

SIGNOR
FRANCESCO DRAGO,
Lord of Riofreddo, etc.

It will be six years since, having published Cartari's Images of the Gods, I honoured them with the name of Your Illustrious Lordship, both to make a name for them, as I indeed managed to do, and to give the world some sign of my great respect for the name of one who had always favoured and protected me. Now, having reprinted the same Images at considerable expense and with the effort of a person very knowledgeable in these matters, and having brought them to a form little short of new, I have not known how to provide them with any other patron and defender than the name of Your Illustrious Lordship. I know that you will welcome in this work, by now familiar to your eyes, the affection of an old servant who professes that everything he is, he has from the kindness of Your Illustrious Lordship, and largely acknowledges it.

And because this new edition comes out enriched with a second part, of images of the deities of the Indies, I have thought it right to give this second part to the friendship that you hold with Signor Piero Buarno [?1], so that, just as likeness of studies and of minds has bound you together, so men may also see outwardly a sign of so noble and honourable a bond, and some trace of gratitude in a person who remembers having received favours both from Your Illustrious Lordship and from Signor Buarno. Because of their greatness, these favours can only be pointed out from afar, with memory and with the desire to do much, where much comes to nothing. I bow to Your Illustrious Lordship and wish you from the Lord the fulfilment of your every desire.

From Padua, 1 November 1614.
Your Illustrious Lordship's
most humble servant
Pietro Paolo Tozzi.

## Doubtful passages

- The description of the printer's mark on scan 0007 (in the source a `[ГРАВЮРА: …]` line) was written during transcription and is not printed text; it is not included in the translation. The mark has no caption.
- “si per dar loro nome, come apunto mi riuscì” (scan 0009): *dar nome* is the set phrase for “to give renown, to make known” (compare “as I indeed managed to do”); *nome* in the neighbouring “col nome di V.S.” means “name”. Rendered “to make a name for them”.
- [?1] “Piero Buarno” (scan 0010): so in the source; possibly a corrupted or Italianised form of the name; no other spelling is found. Rendered “Piero Buarno” [?].
- The date “1 November 1614” stands in an edition of 1626: the dedication is reprinted from an earlier edition (“It will be six years” counts from 1614). The date is given as printed.
- “Dei della gentilità” (scan 0007): *gentilità* is paganism (the world of the *gentili*, that is, the pagans); rendered “paganism”, as in the glossary.
- “Principi” in “the galleries of Princes” follows the glossary (“Principe” → “Prince”).
- Every “V.S. Illustriss.” (*Vostra Signoria Illustrissima*) is rendered “Your Illustrious Lordship”, as in the glossary. “Illustriss.” in the address line (“All'Illustriss. Sig.”) is rendered “Most Illustrious”; “Patron mio Colendissimo” (scan 0009) is rendered “my most honoured patron” (*colendissimo*: most to be honoured, revered).
- “che professa tanto quanto egli è, tutto hauere, & riconoscere in gran parte dalla humanità di V.S. Illustriss.” (scan 0010): construed as “who professes that all he is, he has, and largely acknowledges, from the kindness of Your Illustrious Lordship”; *in gran parte* is taken with *riconoscere*.
- “acciò che … vedano” (scan 0011): a purpose clause, rendered “so that, just as … so men may … see”.
- There are no printer's errors on these scans in the markup.
