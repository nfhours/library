# Review: Pignoria's Addition (en/aggionta.md, scans 0571–0586)

I checked every block of en/aggionta.md against `_text/pages/0571–0586.md` (text, caption on 0583, inscription on 0584, margins on 0571–0574 and 0578), then against ru/aggionta.md. Block numbers follow parse_chapter (1-based). The structure check gives `aggionta: OK`; there are 35 blocks, identical to the RU.

## Fixes (block n)
1. Blocks 14 and 16: "the Author" is now "the author" (E3, and the lower-case form used in every other EN chapter).
2. Block 21: *quelli poi, che succederono* was "Those who succeeded them, however". *Poi* means "afterwards", not "however" (E4), so it now reads "Those who succeeded them afterwards".
3. Block 21: *simbolo molte volte di guerra* was "a symbol many times of war", a calque. It is now "often a symbol of war" (E1).
4. Block 22: *Annotations on the Pandects* is now in italics, as a title, consistent with *Library*, *Preparation for the Gospel* and the other titles in this chapter.
5. Block 24: *o Libera che la vogliamo chiamare* was "as we like to call her", which suggests a habit. The Italian offers an alternative name, so it is now "or Libera, if we would rather call her that". The RU «как нам угодно её называть» is acceptable.
6. Block 26: *nelle cose di Arcadia* was "in the matters of Arcadia", a calque. It is now "in his account of Arcadia". "eighth Book" → "eighth book" (E3).
7. Block 33: *non ho voluto fraudarne i studiosi* was "I did not wish to defraud of it the scholars", an inverted calque. It is now "I did not wish to withhold it from the scholars".
8. Block 34: *al qual Oro spetta ancora la testuggine* was "to which Horus belongs also the tortoise". This was ungrammatical and could be read as "Horus belongs". It is now "and to this Horus the tortoise also belongs".

## Additions queried in the brief
- "my **coin** of Caepio" (block 3). *Nella mia di Cepione* has a feminine possessive with its noun left out (*moneta* or *medaglia*), so English needs a noun. "Coin" is the neutral one, the RU also has «монете», and the Doubtful passages note explains it. Accepted.
- "on **fol.** 188 [?3]" (block 29). The Italian *si vede 188.* is elliptical, and the supplied "fol." is marked [?3] at the same word as the RU. Accepted.
- "His Most Serene **Highness** of Mantua" (block 8). *Sereniss.* is an adjective used as a noun, so English needs a head noun. "His Most Serene Highness" is the standard English style for a ruling duke (the Gonzaga). The alternative, "the Most Serene Duke", would add a fact the text does not state. Accepted.

## Verdict on the translator's doubtful points
- *il vulgo delli Antiquarij* → "the common run of antiquaries" (block 5) is correct and keeps the slight disdain. The RU «большинство антиквариев» is weaker but not wrong.
- *murari antichi* → "ancient wall paintings" (block 34) is acceptable as a conjecture (*murali*). The word could also mean simply "ancient walls" (wall reliefs). The Doubtful passages note marks it, so it can stay.
- *Il Veretro* → "phallus" is correct (*veretrum* = the male member) and matches the glossary-new entry.
- *Gioia* → "gemstone" (vase of gemstone, i.e. carved hardstone) and "gem" (*gioia di Annello* = ring gem) are both correct.
- *ilquale fosse initiato* → "who was initiated" is correct. The imperfect subjunctive describes a state at the time the object was made, and "had been initiated" would add an anteriority the Italian does not mark. Either would be acceptable; I kept it.
- *Tillino* / *Tornax* kept as printed: agreed. Tillinus follows the glossary. The Tournai (*Tornacum*) idea is rightly left to the note and not put into the text.
- Pausanias reported speech (block 26): the chain of "that … that …" clauses follows the Italian *che … & che … ma che … & che*, and everything stays attributed to Pausanias. The one "that" supplied before "before Ceres stood Hercules" fits the sequence. Accepted.
- Πυρφόροι: the accentuation is correct (πυρφόρος, accent on the penult; the plural is πυρφόροι). Adding the accents to the unaccented print is acceptable and is noted.
- [?1] *Aratro* → *Aratus* (Grotius, *Syntagma Arateorum*, Leiden, Raphelengius 1600) and [?2] *contraceuole* → "more fitting" are both agreed. All three [?N] marks sit at the same Italian words as in the RU and match Doubtful passages.
- The margin order on scan 0573 (110, 188, 158, …) is matched to the paragraphs in printed order, as in the RU. Agreed.
- *Fornuto* → Cornutus, *Equità* → Equity, *Bufono* → Bouphonos, *Grutero* → Gruterus (from *Giano Grutero*) all follow the glossary.
