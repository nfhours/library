## Fixes (block n)
- 4: *fa, che ella così gli dice di sè stessa* (E5.2). "Petrarch … has her say this of herself" → "in Petrarch's canzone … she speaks thus to him of herself" (this also restores the missing *gli*, "to him").
- 9 (Juvenal verse, line 2) and 33 (caption): *Nume* = numen. "her divinity" → "her numen"; "this deity" → "this numen".
- 26, 110: titles in italics, *Marriage of Philologia* and Cicero's *Rhetoric*.
- 27 and 64: *stranamente percuoteua / percotendola stranamente* now reads "harshly" (was "strangely"). See the verdict below.
- 53: *Licinio* → "Licinius" (was "Licymnius"). The GLOSSARY entry *Licinio = Licymnius* is for Hercules' cousin, and its own comment says that in "Fortuna" it is a different person: Catullus' friend Licinius (Calvus, Catullus 50 *ne poenas Nemesis reposcat a te*). A line for *Licinio (Catullo)* is added to `fortuna.glossary-new.tsv`.
- 57: "Whose image is described by Chrysippus" was a sentence fragment. It now reads "Her image is described…".
- 64: *volle, che Antifilo … fosse poi sempre suo schiauo*. "his slave" was ambiguous between Ptolemy and Apelles. It now reads "Apelles' slave", as in Lucian and the RU.
- 68: *facendo festa della bellezza sua, & adornandola tuttauia il più, che poteuano*. The calque "making a feast of her beauty and adorning her all the more they could", which was also ungrammatical, now reads "making much of her beauty and adorning her all the while as much as they could".
- 94 (Dante verse, lines 10–11): the English had swapped the two lines. The order is restored: "With more colours, underlaid and overlaid, / there never were Tartar or Turkish cloths,".
- 97: *se pur in altra parte del corpo lo vengono a ferire* is conditional, not concessive. "even if they wound him…" → "if they happen instead to wound him…".
- 102 (Horace verse, line 14): *Però ringratio lei* → "So I thank her" (was "Yet"). *Però* means "therefore" here. The chapter translates it as "so" everywhere else (the Envy verse, the Pacuvius verse), and the RU has «Потому».
- 102 (lines 17–19): "and my thoughts too" → "and that my thoughts too". See the verdict below.
- 123: *Posidippo mette … vn rasoio in mano al suo, & Ausonio alla sua dà la Penitenza*. "puts a razor in the hand of his, and Ausonius gives his Repentance" read as if the Repentance belonged to Ausonius. It now reads "in the hand of his god … gives his goddess Repentance besides as a companion".
- 127: *giunga … con noi mano a mano* means "hand in hand", not "hand to hand" (which suggests fighting). It now reads "joins us hand in hand". *le penne, ch'ella vi hà d'intorno* means the feathers around her hands, so "about her" → "around her hands".
- 128 (caption): Occasion is "she" everywhere else in the chapter, so "it flies away" → "she flies away".
- 148: *venendo a mercato di quello* was a calque, "coming to market over". It now reads "bargaining over".

## Translator's doubtful points: verdict
- **Giraldi, *come è stata questa parimente* (152).** The verb *è stata* echoes *siano state dipinture moderne*, so the more natural reading is "as this one too was [modern]", which Giraldi's own *a' tempi nostri* confirms. The RU «как не нахожу и той» supplies "I find no mention" instead. That reading is grammatically possible, since *stata* could agree with *mentione*, and both readings give the same facts. So it is not a real error of meaning. The English "as was likewise the case with this one" keeps the ambiguity of the Italian and is left as it is.
- **Dante's Fraud verse, line 6 (94).** The print has *E d'Vn serpente l'Vno, e l'altro fusto*, where Dante has *tutto l'altro fusto*. Rule 8 says to translate what is printed, so the English "and a serpent's were both parts of the trunk" is right. The RU follows Dante's text, not the print (recorded in RU errors).
- **"stranamente percuoteua" (27, and 64).** In sixteenth-century usage *stranamente* means "harshly, cruelly, beyond measure" (compare Boccaccio's *stranamente battere*), not "oddly". Both places now read "harshly". The RU has «странным образом» in 27 (error) and «жестоко» in 64 (correct).
- **Horace verse, lines 17–19 (102).** Both negatives sit inside the *che* clause governed by *non sì*: "not so much that I do not remember my own, and that my thoughts too have no firm hope there". Here *v'* means "in my own things", which matches Horace's *mea virtute me involvo*. The English line was correct, but it could be read as a separate statement, so "that" was added to make it parallel. The RU «и чтобы твёрдой надежды на них не имели и мои мысли» reads it the same way and is correct.
- **[?1] *fede/sede* (32).** Agreed: *fede* is a misprint for *sede*, "seat". The mark is at the first "seat", which is the same Italian word as the RU mark.
- **[?2] (62).** The explanation is agreed. The print has a comma after *spada nuda* and a capital on *Scriue*, and the RU block boundary (62/63) falls at the same point. The mark is at "naked sword", as in the RU.
- ***Liuore, cioè la Inuidia* (68).** The English uses the glossary form, "Malice". The parenthesis "(*Liuore*)" is acceptable under E5.5, because the author is glossing the word itself (*cioè*). It matches the RU «Злоба (*Liuore*), то есть Зависть». Kept.
