# Vertumnus

Source: books/cartari/_text/TEXT_reading.md, scans 0270–0271
Rules: books/cartari/_translation/en/RULES.md

---

> What do you marvel at, seeing
> so many forms in one head? If you listen to me,
> you will be able to learn that I am Vertumnus.
> I came here from Tuscany, where by many
> I am not visited, nor did they ever give me
> temples, with arches or with magnificent vaults [?1].
> Of this I take no heed at all, because it is
> enough for me to look upon the Roman Forum,
> and I never cared for any other honour.
> Through here, in their course, there once passed
> the waters of the Tiber, as is said,
> which afterwards were turned (*voltate*) another way.
> For the fair Tiber, with a joyful and happy
> outcome, wished to make room for his people,
> and this was the root of my name.
> Or else from the year, which little by little
> goes turning (*volgendo*), I was called Vertumnus,
> and consecrated also in this place,
> as if through me, under the humble roof,
> the countryman stores the harvest,
> which he afterwards enjoys, and for this reason
> you see that I am surrounded by many
> grapes, which turn purple, and my head
> is wholly wound with ripe ears of grain.
> And it seems that time every year clothes me anew,
> according to the season, with sweet fruits,
> which a hand, ready for my honour, holds out to me.
> So here you see the apples already produced
> by the pear tree in spite of itself, which the skilful
> grafter offered me, and of all the
> others I do not now wish to tell you, because, led [?2]
> by false rumour, I bring you
> anew yet another reason for my name.
> But you, believe not what people say
> of me, but what I myself say,
> for not all tongues are good for the truth.
> My nature is fit, as you see,
> to transform itself into all shapes:
> put me in a chariot, on horseback, or show me on foot.
> I suit myself to everything, and if you care
> to see me as a delicate young girl,
> give me women's clothes, clean and pure,
> I shall be a man, if the toga is given me,
> and with the sickle I shall be a reaper,
> if I have my brow crowned with hay.
> Clad in arms, I have already earned no little honour
> by them: so it seemed
> to everyone that I was a man of great valour;
> and whoever afterwards takes the arms from about me
> and dresses me as a grave litigant,
> I seem to him born for lawsuits; and if it is burdensome
> to see me so severe, feasting,
> almost drunk you shall see me, if you adorn my head
> with roses, and singing joyfully and merrily.
> Then I shall seem Bacchus to you, if you adorn me
> with the mitre that he wears, and you will swear
> that in all your days you have never seen
> one who more resembles Phoebus, if you give me
> the bow and the cithara. And a great hunter
> you will believe me, if I have the nets.
> Whoever sees a light reed in my hand
> will call me a charming fowler, like Faunus;
> and what? Do I not have the heart
> to show myself to you next as well, as
> a skilled charioteer, like one who guides
> the running horses with a strong hand?
> In short, there is neither limit nor law
> to my changing into various forms,
> which I do so well that no one ever corrects it.
> If I wish, I shall be like one who watches
> the tracks of the roaming flocks and herds,
> or I shall make myself like a fisherman.
> And the thing that, perhaps most of all, makes you hear
> me often named is that the fine fruits
> of well-tilled gardens are always present to me:
> such as the gourd, and the cabbage bound with twisted
> rushes, and the watermelons, which are
> brought to me, also mark me out.
> And in conclusion I tell you that all that adorns and fills with flowers
> the joyful meadows is all given to me.
> And because I turn (*riuolto*) now and again
> into very many forms, I was called Vertumnus.
>
> *(Propertius)*

## Doubtful passages

- [?1] “temples, with arches or with magnificent vaults” (scan 0270, “Tempi, con archi, ò con superbi volti”) — *volti* may be “vaults” (as translated) or “faces”; translated as “vaults”, which suits the temples and arches.
- [?2] “because, led by false rumour” (scan 0270, “perche scorto da la mendace fama”) — the construction of *scorto* is not entirely clear: Vertumnus either “is guided” by false rumour to bring another reason for his name, or is “aware of” it; translated as “led”.
