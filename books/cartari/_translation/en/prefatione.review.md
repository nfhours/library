# Review: prefatione (English)

Block structure checked against ru/prefatione.md (10 blocks, no asides, no quotations); `[?1]`, `[?2]` in place. Checker: `prefatione: OK`.

## English fixes

1. Block 4: "my Master" -> "my teacher" ("mio Maestro"; E3, no Italian-style capital; sense is teacher).
2. Block 4: "has set forth with a learned commentary" -> "has elucidated with a learned commentary" ("ha dechiarato" = explained, not published).
3. Blocks 4, 5: "goes about / go about" -> "circulates / circulate" ("va in volta", "vanno in volta"; "go about" is unidiomatic for books).
4. Block 5: "murdered ... in representing in figures what he had expressed" -> "in the rendering into figures of what he had expressed": Valeriano is not the one drawing; the English wrongly made him the subject of "representing".
5. Block 6: "a most gracious composition" -> "a most elegant composition" ("componimento gentilissimo"; English "gracious" = kind/courteous; consistent with "most elegant figures" in block 5).
6. Block 7: "a learned and gracious work" -> "a learned and elegant work" ("opera dotta e gentile"), same reason.
7. Block 8: omission restored: "E veramente nell'Autore" -> "And indeed, in the author..." ("veramente" had been dropped).

## Russian errors

- Block 8: «А в Авторе, которого мы держим в руках» drops «veramente» («и в самом деле / поистине»).

## Open questions for the author

- Block 10: "E ben vero, ch'io credo, che la Modestia non si lamentarà di me" is rendered as a concession ("It is true, however, I believe, that..."), as in the Russian («Правда, я думаю»). It could also be an affirmation ("I do truly believe that Modesty will not complain of me"). The concessive reading was kept.
- Block 10: "A Dio" is kept as "God be with you" (it matches the Russian «С Богом»). "Farewell" would be the more usual English letter closing. Which do you prefer?
- Block 4: the glossary prescribes *Notitia Dignitatum*, but the Italian has only "Notitia dell'Imperio Orientale, & Occidentale". The English follows the glossary, which adds "Dignitatum". "the *Notitia* of the Eastern and Western Empire" would be closer to the text.
- Block 7: "E però Abramo Gorleo" is translated "And so" (old "però" = therefore), the same as the Russian. The logical link to the Orsini sentence is loose, but that is how it is printed.
