# Catalogue of the Hundred Most Famous Gods of the Ancients

**Catalogue of the hundred most famous gods of the ancients, with their nature and properties, drawn from Cartari and other authors who treat of this subject.**

*By Cesare Malfatti of Padua.*

1 Eternity, goddess of everlastingness and of immortality.

2 Nature, goddess of production and begetter of all things.

3 Adad and Adargatis, among the Assyrians gods of the production and generation of all things.

4 Uranus, universal father of the gods, god of heaven and of the firmament.

5 Cybele, or Ops, called the universal Great Mother of the gods and of men, goddess of the earth.

6 Saturn, god of time and of the poor, father of Jupiter, Juno, Neptune and Pluto.

7 Jupiter, god of the gods of the ancients, hurling lightning and thundering, presiding over hospitality and over giving aid.

8 Mars, god of war, of valour and of bravery.

9 Apolline, also called Phoebus and the Sun, god of the light of day, of poets, of foretelling and of divining.

10 Venus, goddess of beauty, of lust and of lasciviousness.

11 Mercury, messenger of the gods, god of eloquence, of thieves and of merchants.

12 Diana, goddess of the woods, of hunters, of chastity and of virginity.

13 Matuta, or Aurora, goddess of the dawn and of the first light of day.

14 Vesta, goddess of the element of fire and of the sacred Vestal virgins.

15 Juno, wife and sister of Jupiter, goddess of the air, of marriages, of kingdoms and of childbirths.

16 Neptune and Amphitrite his wife, gods and rulers of the sea.

17 Glaucus, sea god of majestic appearance, the first of the sea gods after Neptune.

18 Thetis, sea goddess, the most beautiful of all the goddesses after Venus, and the first of the sea gods after Neptune, Amphitrite and Glaucus.

19 Portunus, also called Palaemon, god of harbours and of the safe return home of sailors.

20 Castor — Brothers, sons of Jupiter, gods of sailors, of  
21 Pollux — the calm of the sea, and of horses.

21 Isis among the Egyptians, called Io by the Greeks, goddess of sailors.

23 Aeolus, god of the winds, and of storms at sea and on land.

14 Ceres, the Eleusinian goddess, inventor of grain and of the cultivation of the fields.

25 Bacchus of Thebes, god of wine and its inventor, and inventor of the triumph, of pastimes and of diversions.

26 Bona Dea, keeper of the seeds and of the fertility of all things.

27 Priapus, god of kitchen gardens, of generative nature and of seed.

28 Vertumnus, god of kitchen gardens and of gardens, and also of human thoughts.

29 Pomona, wife of Vertumnus, goddess of kitchen gardens and of gardens, cultivator of plants.

30 Flora, goddess of flowers and of loveliness, and of the season of spring.

31 Pan, god of shepherds and of flocks, inventor of the flute.

32 Plutus, a boy, god of riches and of treasures.

33 Vulcan, god of material and earthly fire, maker of thunderbolts for Jupiter.

34 Pluto, god of the underworld, of the damned, of torments and of riches.

35 Proserpina, wife of Pluto, goddess of the underworld, of the damned, and of the fertility of the earth.

36 Charon, god, ferryman of the underworld; on the river Acheron he ferries across the souls of the damned.

37 Judges of the underworld, three; and the first, Minos, examines the souls about their guilt.

38 Aeacus reads the trial records drawn up against the souls of the damned.

39 Rhadamanthus records the sentences given against the damned by those three judges.

40 Nemesis, goddess of punishment for the wicked, and one who shows good to the good.

41 Alecto — These are the three goddesses, or Furies of the underworld,  
42 Tisiphone — inciting mortals to the greatest wickedness, and then the wicked  
43 Megaera — harshly punishing them in the underworld; they are called the long-haired goddesses.

44 The Parcae, three; the first is Clotho, who spins the thread of life.

45 Lachesis winds the thread of life on the reel.

46 Atropos cuts and severs the thread of life; these three are sisters, and they dwell in the underworld.

47 Truth, goddess of the true, and discoverer of falsehood; she is the strongest of all things.

48 Virtue, goddess of good and generous deeds, giver and bringer of every good.

49 Justice, goddess of reward and of punishment, bringer of good to the good, and chastiser of the wicked.

50 Honour, god of fame, of glory and of immortality.

51 Rumour, goddess, bringer of good and bad outcomes, keeper of human actions.

52 Victory, goddess of conquering and of overcoming others, and goddess of glory.

53 Concord, goddess of peace, union and love, and goddess of the well-being of all things.

54 Peace, goddess of quiet and of rest, of abundance and of the multiplication of all things.

55 Macaria, daughter of the god Hercules, goddess of happiness.

56 Fidius [?1] of faith and fidelity, and of the keeping of pacts and promises.

57 Anubis, also called Serapis, god of time, of production, of sagacity, of guardianship and of fidelity.

58 Silence, also called Harpocrates, god of secrecy and of taciturnity.

59 Terminus, god of stability.

60 Antevorta and Postvorta, goddesses who know [?2] the past and the future.

61 Genii, gods of souls and of quiet, observers of the human race and of its actions.

62 Lares, gods, guardians of provinces, places, cities and houses, investigators of human deeds.

63 Penates, household gods, guardians of provinces, places, cities and houses, like the Lares gods.

64 Portunus, god of doors, keys and bolts.

65 Fortune, goddess of human actions, lady and patroness of everything, most powerful of all the gods.

66 Necessity, goddess of fate and of destiny.

67 Iris, messenger of the gods, and in particular of the goddess Juno.

68 Hebe, daughter of Juno, cupbearer of the gods, goddess of youth and of liberty.

69 Pallas, goddess of wisdom, inventor of the good arts, and goddess of war.

70 Maia, mother of the god Mercury, goddess of responses, and inciter of battles.

71 Bellona, goddess of war, and charioteer of Mars, the warlike god.

72 Hercules, god of fortitude, tamer of monsters and of tyrants.

73 Palaestra, daughter of the god Mercury, goddess of wrestlers.

74 Peitho, goddess of the tongue and of eloquence.

75 Aesculapius, son of Apolline, god of medicine.

76 Health, goddess of soundness of body and deliverer from infirmity.

77 Janus, two-faced god, god of peace and of civil life, protector of the Italians.

78 Momus, god of slander, reproach and murmuring.

79 Discord, goddess of quarrels, ill will, hatreds, evil events and ruins.

80 Cupid, or Amor, son of Venus, god of loving, of good will, and of propagation.

81 Anteros, brother of Cupid, god of loving in return, and of reciprocal love.

82 The Graces, three; the first of them is Euphrosyne, who presides over gladness and cheerfulness, and bestows the benefit.

83 Aglaia presides over majesty and loveliness, and receives the benefit.

84 Thalia presides over affability, and returns the benefit; these three are the goddesses of beauty, of gratitude and of the seasons of the year.

85 Hymenaeus, god of marriage and of weddings.

86 Parthenope — Three Siren goddesses who entice to  
87 Leucosia — lasciviousness, and who also  
88 Ligeia — punish.

89 Volupia, goddess of pleasures and of voluptuousness.

90 Angerona, goddess of pleasures and of human undertakings, and goddess of the throat.

91 Sleep, god of sleep, rest and quiet, and of shadows.

92 The Muses, nine; and the first, Clio, presides over history, assigned to the Moon.

93 Euterpe presides over all the sciences in general, assigned to Mercury.

94 Thalia presides over music, comedies and memory, assigned to Venus.

95 Melpomene presides over harmony and tragedies, assigned to the Sun.

96 Terpsichore presides over poetic frenzy, inventor of the psaltery, assigned to Mars.

97 Erato presides over amorous matters and over geometry, assigned to Jupiter.

98 Polyhymnia presides over rhetoric, the oratorical art, and verse, assigned to Saturn.

99 Urania presides over astrology and is its inventor, assigned to Uranus, or to Heaven.

190 Calliope presides over heroic verse, assigned as superior to all the others, as the noblest.

These nine Muses, with Apolline their master, are called patronesses and inventors of music and of all the other sciences and arts.

*These are the hundred gods who were of greater name and more famous among the ancients, as is known from the poets as well as from the historians and painters.*

## Doubtful passages

- The running numbers are printed with errors, which are kept: after 20 and 21 (Castor, Pollux), Isis again has the number "21" (it should be 22); Ceres is printed under "14" (it should be 24); Calliope under "190" (it should be 100). The other numbers run in sequence.
- Scan 0018: in the right margin beside item 15 the printer set "(Mare", which is the end of item 16 ("…imperatori del Mare"), moved sideways for lack of room in the line. It is not an authorial marginal note, so item 16 is given whole: "gods and rulers of the sea".
- Items 20–21, 41–43 and 86–88 are joined in the book by a curly brace with one shared explanation; in the translation the explanation is divided among the lines as in the book.
- [?1] "Fidio della Fede" (item 56): the word "Dio" ("god") is not in the text, so "della Fede" has no noun to qualify; it was probably dropped by the printer (Dio Fidio, Dius Fidius, is meant). Rendered "Fidius [?1] of faith and fidelity", without adding "god".
- [?2] "Anteuorta, & Postuorta, dee che fanno il passato, & l'auenire" (item 60): the source has *fanno* ("make, do"), which does not fit the sense. In the chapter on Janus ("Anteuorta, e Postuorta compagne della Diuinità, quella perche sapeua l'auenire, questa il passato") Antevorta is said to have known the future and Postvorta the past. Probably a misprint for *sanno* ("know"); rendered "know [?2]".
- "Dea della gola" (item 90): *gola* here means "throat", not "gluttony": in the chapter on Angerona she delivered Rome from a disease of the throat (*angina*), and the caption of her engraving calls her goddess "sopra il male della gola". Rendered "goddess of the throat".
- "Apolline" (items 9, 75 and the closing paragraph): the old form of the name of Apollo; rendered "Apolline" throughout, as in the text, following the glossary.
- "Pluto fanciullo" (item 32): this is Plutus, the god of riches, not Pluto, as the word "fanciullo" ("boy") shows; rendered "Plutus" per the glossary.
- "Agalia" (item 83): a corrupted form of the name Aglaia; rendered "Aglaia". "Leucasia" (item 87): *Leucosia*, rendered "Leucosia" per the glossary. "Tersicore" (item 96): rendered "Terpsichore" per the glossary.
- "Tethide" (item 18): a variant spelling of *Thetide*; rendered "Thetis" per the glossary: the text describes her as the most beautiful sea goddess after Venus, i.e. the Nereid Thetis, mother of Achilles (not Tethys, wife of Oceanus).
- "Cesare Malfatto" (heading, scan 0017): on the title page (scan 0007) he is "Malfatti"; here it is printed differently, and rendered "Malfatti" per the glossary.
- "Matuta ouero Aurora, Dea dell'Alba, & dell'Albore" (item 13): *Albore* is "the first light of dawn"; rendered "of the dawn and of the first light of day".
- "crinite" (item 43): "Dee crinite" means "long-haired, with a mane of hair" (of the snake-haired Furies); rendered "long-haired".
- "inaspa" (item 45): *inaspare* is to wind yarn on a reel; rendered "winds on the reel".
- "Responsi" (item 70): *responsi* means "responses" (of seers, of oracles); rendered "responses", without adding "oracular". "concitratrice" is rendered "inciter".
- "seragli" (item 64): here "bolts, bars" (not "menagerie"); rendered "bolts".
- "Fortezza" (item 72): *fortezza* means fortitude, steadfastness; rendered "fortitude".
- "sopra" in items 7 and 82–84 and 92–99 ("sopra la maestà", "sopra l'historia", etc.): "presides over, has charge of"; rendered "presides over" (in item 7, "presiding over").
- Printer's misprints with no effect on the sense (silently corrected in the translation): "Cercre" (Cerere), "Verita" (Verità), "Vittorria" (Vittoria), "tacurnità" (taciturnità), "Volutà" (Voluttà), "moglie d Vertunno" in item 29 (a letter is missing: "d" for "di"), "Eolo Dio de Venti." (a full stop for a comma), "letre Dee" (*le tre Dee*, item 41), "apo" (*appo*, item 22), "Necessita" (*Necessità*, item 66), "concitratrice" (*concitatrice*, item 70); the spelling "Hospitio" (hospitio) is old-fashioned, rendered "hospitality".
- Capitalization ("Dio/dio", "Dea/dea", "Custodi", "Città", "Prouincie") follows English usage (E3) and is lowercase, except for names and personifications; in the book, from item 59 on, the author more often writes "dio" and "dea" in lower case. Other renderings: *orti* "kitchen gardens", *giardini* "gardens", *fà il benefitio / riceue / rende* "bestows / receives / returns the benefit"; "Fortuna" (item 65) is rendered "Fortune" as the name of a goddess in the list, not the general power "fortune"; "Cupidine ò Amore" (item 80) is rendered "Cupid, or Amor" to keep the two names, and "fratello d'Amore" (item 81) "brother of Cupid" per the glossary (Amore = Cupid); "ad Vranio ouero al Cielo" (item 99) "Uranus, or Heaven", with *Cielo* capitalized as the alternative name of the same god and sphere.
